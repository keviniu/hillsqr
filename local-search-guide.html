Local search guide

Why your business shows up in your own town but not the towns around it

Why it happens, why listing 30 towns doesn't help, and what works instead.

Many local businesses ask us the same two questions:

  1. “Why do I show up when people search in my own town, but not in the other towns I cover?”
  2. “Should I list 30 nearby towns on my website or Google profile?”

Why it happens

For local searches, Google mainly weighs three things:

  • Distance: how close your business address is to the person searching. This is the biggest factor.
  • Relevance: how well your profile and website match what they searched for.
  • Prominence: how well known and trusted you are, mostly through reviews and links from other sites.

Your service areas tell Google where you’re willing to work. They don’t move your ranking there. A business based in the next town over starts with a distance advantage, even if you also cover that town.

Should you list 30 towns?

No. Longer lists don’t improve the result, and they can make things worse.

  • Google Business Profile allows up to 20 service areas. Choose the towns you would really travel to, starting with the closest.
  • On your website, don’t paste dozens of town names into the footer or the text. Google treats this as keyword stuffing and can lower your whole site.
  • Don’t make 30 near-identical pages with only the town name changed. Google ignores them or demotes them.

What works instead

1

Real pages for your best towns

Pick three to five towns where you have customers or want them. Give each its own page with real content: the jobs you’ve done nearby, local questions and photos. A few good pages beat dozens of copies with the town name swapped.

2

Reviews that mention the work and the area

Ask each happy customer for a Google review. Details such as “great IT support in Crowthorne” help Google connect your business with that town.

No one can promise a particular ranking. Google’s local results change, and ranking in towns you aren’t based in takes time.

These two take time and care to get right.
