Case study

Getting a roofing company's Google Local Services Ads working

A local roofing company had set aside a sizeable weekly budget for Google Local Services Ads, but nothing was being spent and no leads were coming in. This is what we reviewed, what changed, and the early results.

The problem

The business had a generous weekly budget for Local Services Ads, Google's pay-per-lead advertising for local trades. Weeks went by and nothing was charged, and nobody could see why.

Local Services Ads charge per lead, not per view or click. So no charges meant one thing: no leads were reaching the business. The advertising was set up and the money was available, but it wasn't turning into enquiries.

What we did

Over two days we reviewed the three things a local customer and Google both look at, and updated each of them:

  • Local Services Ads settings: how the account was set up and what it was asking Google to show.
  • Google Business Profile: the listing customers see in Search and Maps.
  • Website home page: where the customer lands and decides whether to get in touch.

These three work together. A strong ad that sends people to a weak profile or home page won't win enquiries, and the reverse is also true.

What the figures show

The ads are now shown as the very first result more than three times as often, while still appearing above unpaid results almost every time. Daily visibility has held steady at around eight or nine impressions a day.

What we can't say yet

These are early, small numbers from only a few days, and there are no charged leads yet. That means the original problem of money set aside but no enquiries is not yet solved, and we won't claim it is. Better positioning is a good first sign, because customers can only call a business they can see. What matters is whether it turns into leads.
